# How to read this book {.unnumbered}

## Three routes {.unnumbered}

| What you want to sort out | Where to read | Roughly how many pages |
|:--|:--|--:|
| What artificial intelligence is actually about | Part 1 | 150 |
| Why the development toolchain looks the way it does | Part 2 (from 2.1) | 70 |
| Build the complete picture | The whole book, in order | 230 |

Part 2 does not depend on Part 1. Part 1 does have internal dependencies: each section names its prerequisites at the start, and following that chain all the way back gives you all the background you need.

## The fixed structure of each section {.unnumbered}

Every section unfolds in the same six-beat rhythm:

1. **The one-line intuition** — no jargon, just say the thing in plain words first.
2. **An everyday analogy** — a scene you have lived through yourself.
3. **The diagram** — look at the picture before the explanation.
4. **The problem it solves** — why history needed this thing, and what happens without it.
5. **Its built-in limitations** — the most important part of the section. Every weakness is derived from the principle, and the most common misconception gets named.
6. **Summary and self-check** — three sentences of summary, two questions to test yourself.

A fixed structure has one side effect: on a second reading you can go through steps 1 and 5 only, and a whole book shrinks to two sheets of paper.

## On staying current {.unnumbered}

Technical details go stale. The book's rule is: what will still hold in three years goes in the main text, what will not goes in a dated note box. So you will see qualifiers like "as of such-and-such a month" — those are not disclaimers, they are part of the information itself.

## On terminology {.unnumbered}

The first time a term appears, the English original is given too, for example "gradient descent". This is not decoration: anyone who wants to look further needs to know what to search for.
